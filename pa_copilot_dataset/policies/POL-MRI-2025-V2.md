# Lumbar Spine MRI (POL-MRI-2025-V2)
Version 2 | Effective 2025-07-01 to 2026-03-31 | Plans: PPO-GOLD, HMO-SILVER

## 1. Scope and applicability
1.1 This policy applies to MRI of the lumbar spine (CPT 72148, 72149, 72158) for adult members.
1.2 Applicable plans: PPO-GOLD, HMO-SILVER.
1.3 Governing date. The policy version in effect on the date the authorization request is received governs the review, regardless of the scheduled service date.

## 2. Medical necessity criteria
All of the following must be met unless the red flag pathway (Section 3) or an exception (Section 5) applies.
- **C-01** Low back pain has persisted for at least 6 weeks (42 days), with the onset date documented.
- **C-02** At least 6 weeks of conservative therapy (physical therapy, home exercise program, and/or NSAIDs or analgesics).
- **C-03** A clinical examination (musculoskeletal and neurological) by the treating clinician is documented within 60 days before the request date.
- **C-04** No lumbar spine MRI or CT was performed in the prior 12 months, unless a new or progressive neurological deficit has been documented since that imaging.

## 3. Red flag pathway
3.1 RF-01. Any of: new or progressive motor deficit, suspected cauda equina syndrome, suspected spinal infection, or suspected malignancy. When RF-01 is documented, C-01 and C-02 are waived. C-03 still applies.

## 4. Exclusions
4.1 EX-01. Routine imaging for non-specific low back pain without red flags before the minimum symptom and treatment durations are met.
4.2 EX-02. Imaging requested solely at patient request without clinical indication.

## 5. Exceptions
5.1 EXC-003 Back Care Pilot. Members enrolled in the 2025 Back Care Pilot need only 21 days of conservative therapy for C-02. Valid 2025-01-01 through 2025-12-31 only.

## 6. Rationale
6.1 Lumbar spine MRI for low back pain is considered medically necessary when low back pain persists despite a trial of conservative treatment, including physical therapy and anti-inflammatory medication, and when the treating clinician has documented a recent examination. Lumbar spine MRI is not indicated for acute low back pain, for low back pain that has responded to conservative treatment, or for routine evaluation of non-specific low back pain without red flags. Persistent low back pain after conservative treatment, with documented examination and without recent imaging, supports lumbar spine MRI.
